# Who Holds the Keys? AI, Power and Blind Spots in Property Markets

Artificial intelligence is reshaping property markets by changing how prices are set, housing advice is delivered and research priorities are formed. This talk examines three challenges: algorithmic pricing power, discrimination in AI-generated housing recommendations and blind spots in AI-assisted research.

First, evidence from US rental markets distinguishes efficiency gains from responsive algorithmic pricing and potential anticompetitive effects arising from shared pricing algorithms. Recent antitrust litigation highlights the importance of data pooling and market coordination. Second, a new audit of large language models (LLMs) examines housing recommendations for financially identical applicants differing in social class, ethnic origin and migration status. Across 10,376 model responses in three UK cities, social class produces the largest disparities in recommended neighbourhoods, while recent migrants are substantially more likely to be advised to rent rather than buy. The findings also reveal a methodological challenge: models may recognise discrimination audits and adjust their behaviour accordingly. Third, an analysis of AI-generated research proposals shows that LLMs concentrate on a narrow range of familiar topics, potentially overlooking important areas of real estate finance and economics.

Together, these findings demonstrate that predictive accuracy does not guarantee fairness, competition or intellectual diversity. Effective AI governance in property markets requires scrutiny of data sharing, systematic discrimination testing and critical evaluation of AI-assisted research, supported by continuous monitoring across markets and model generations.
